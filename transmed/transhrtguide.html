Section 01

What is prolactin?

Prolactin (PRL) is a peptide hormone produced by lactotroph cells in the anterior pituitary gland. It is best known for stimulating milk production after childbirth, but it plays a much broader role: it influences immune function, metabolism, breast tissue development, and reproductive signalling in all people. PMC12148852

For transfeminine people on estrogen therapy, prolactin is one of the most important hormones to track. Estrogen directly stimulates lactotroph cells, which is why your prolactin is expected to rise on HRT — but the degree matters. The goal is to understand what's normal, what to watch, and when to act.

Why prolactin rises on HRT

The short version: estrogen is the primary driver. It acts directly on lactotroph cells in the pituitary via estrogen receptor-α, increasing both prolactin gene expression and the size of the lactotroph population. PMC2646526

For monotherapy users (no antiandrogen): the rise is purely estrogen-driven and typically modest. The steepest increases are seen with cyproterone acetate (CPA), which adds a progestogenic dopamine-suppressing mechanism on top. PMC12148852

Section 02

How estrogen raises prolactin

This is the biology behind why your number changed. Understanding the pathway helps you interpret your results rather than just reacting to them.

1
💉
Estradiol enters circulation
After your EEn injection, estradiol is cleaved from the enanthate ester and enters systemic circulation. Peak levels typically occur 2–4 days post-injection for enanthate. Estradiol binds estrogen receptor-α (ERα) throughout the body, including in the pituitary gland. PMC2646526
2
🧬
Lactotrophs respond
Estradiol binds ERα on lactotroph cells in the anterior pituitary. This activates the PRL gene promoter via Sp1/C/EBPβ transcription factors, increasing both prolactin synthesis and secretion. Estradiol also amplifies the amplitude of prolactin pulses — producing more prolactin per pulse, not more pulses. ScienceDirect
3
📈
Gradual plateau over months
Prolactin rises most steeply in the first 3 months of HRT, then typically stabilises. On estrogen monotherapy, the rise is limited by the absence of the additional dopamine-blocking effect that CPA adds. Levels usually stabilise well below the threshold of clinical concern. PMC12148852

Why monotherapy (your regimen) is different from CPA

Cyproterone acetate (CPA) has a second mechanism: it reduces hypothalamic dopamine signalling. Since dopamine is the primary inhibitor of prolactin secretion, blocking it significantly amplifies the rise. Studies consistently show CPA produces 3–5× higher prolactin elevations than estrogen alone or estrogen + spironolactone. PMC12148852

On EEn monotherapy, your dopamine inhibition is intact. Your prolactin rise is driven purely by estrogen acting on lactotrophs — a fundamentally different (and less extreme) pathway. Your 94.5 ng/mL at month 3 is within what the research would predict for this mechanism.

Section 04

What the levels mean

✓
Normal — no action needed
<28 ng/mL (<600 mIU/L)
Within or near the female reference range. Common early in HRT or on low/moderate estrogen dosing. No monitoring change needed beyond your standard schedule. PMC12148852
⚠
Elevated — monitor closely
28–100 ng/mL (600–2120 mIU/L)
Expected territory on estrogen therapy, especially monotherapy with good E2 levels. On estrogen monotherapy without CPA, hyperprolactinaemia above 600 mIU/L was not reported in a prospective cohort of 882 trans individuals. PMC12148852

Action: Check for symptoms. Discuss with prescriber at next appointment. Continue scheduled bloodwork every 3–6 months.
⚑
Significantly elevated — investigate
>100 ng/mL (>2120 mIU/L)
Levels above 100 ng/mL begin to raise concern for a prolactinoma or macroprolactinaemia. On estrogen monotherapy, reaching this level is unusual and warrants prompt discussion with your prescribing clinician. HealthMatters

Action: Contact prescriber. Your clinician may order an anterior pituitary hormone panel and consider pituitary MRI.
🚨
Critical — MRI indicated
>200 ng/mL (>3000–4000 mIU/L)
At this level, most cases involve a prolactinoma. Clinical guidelines recommend an anterior pituitary hormone panel and pituitary MRI. This is rare on estrogen monotherapy. PMC12148852

Action: Contact your prescriber or endocrinologist urgently. Prolactinomas respond well to dopamine agonists (bromocriptine/cabergoline).
🔬
Macroprolactinaemia — a common false alarm
Some people have naturally elevated prolactin readings caused by large, biologically inactive prolactin complexes (macroprolactin). If your prolactin is elevated without symptoms, your prescriber can order a polyethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation test to rule this out. PMC12148852
Section 05

Symptoms of elevated prolactin

Numbers alone don't tell the whole story. These are the symptoms your prescriber will ask about. Having elevated prolactin without symptoms is common and often requires only monitoring.

Galactorrhoea
Unexpected milk or discharge from nipples. Most specific symptom of true hyperprolactinaemia.
Visual field changes
Tunnel vision or peripheral vision loss. Sign of a large prolactinoma pressing on the optic chiasm.
Persistent headaches
Especially new-onset, frontal or periorbital. Less specific but relevant alongside other symptoms.
Decreased libido
High prolactin suppresses GnRH, which can reduce libido. Confounded by other HRT effects.
Sexual dysfunction
Erectile changes or difficulty with orgasm, beyond what's expected from T suppression alone.
Bone density concerns
Chronic hyperprolactinaemia can impair bone density over years. Relevant for long-term monitoring.

Key point: If you have no symptoms — no galactorrhoea, no vision changes, no unexplained headaches — an elevated prolactin number in the monitored range is not an emergency. Bring it to your prescriber and continue your standard monitoring schedule.

Section 07

Common questions

My prolactin went up — does that mean HRT is working?

A rise in prolactin is consistent with estrogen being active in your body, because estrogen directly stimulates lactotroph cells in the pituitary. So in a loose sense, yes — it's a sign estrogen is reaching target tissue. However, it is not a reliable proxy for overall feminisation progress. Estradiol and testosterone levels are the primary markers of whether your HRT is achieving its goals.

A rise from 31 to 94.5 ng/mL over 3 months on EEn monotherapy is within the expected range of an estrogen-driven response and is not a clinical concern at this level.

Will prolactin keep rising forever, or does it plateau?

On estrogen monotherapy, prolactin typically rises steeply in the first 3 months, then stabilises or plateaus. This is consistent with the prospective cohort data showing the most significant change is in the first 3 months, with non-significant change thereafter on estradiol without CPA. PMC12148852

Your month-3 result is likely close to where your steady state will be, assuming no major dose change.

What's the difference between prolactinoma and HRT-elevated prolactin?

A prolactinoma is a benign tumour of lactotroph cells in the pituitary that secretes prolactin autonomously — independent of your estrogen levels. HRT-elevated prolactin is driven by estrogen stimulating normal lactotroph function.

Most prolactinomas produce prolactin above 200 ng/mL; large ones often exceed 500 ng/mL. Medication-driven elevation rarely exceeds 100 ng/mL on estrogen monotherapy. HealthMatters

Should I test prolactin at peak or trough?

For injectable estradiol, the standard practice is to test at trough — just before your next scheduled injection. Prolactin is not injection-timing-sensitive the way estradiol is; it reflects steady-state pituitary output. However, testing consistently reduces noise in your longitudinal data. BUMC

My labs are in ng/mL but this page uses mIU/L — what's the conversion?

The conversion is: 1 ng/mL × 21.2 = mIU/L. So 94.5 ng/mL equals approximately 2003 mIU/L. Some labs use mU/L — this is the same unit. Always use the reference range printed on your own lab report. HealthMatters

Can I reduce my prolactin by changing my dose?

On estrogen monotherapy, prolactin elevation is dose-dependent. However, you should never self-adjust your injectable estrogen dose to manage prolactin unless instructed by your prescriber. Reducing your E2 dose may compromise testosterone suppression, which is the primary goal of your protocol.
