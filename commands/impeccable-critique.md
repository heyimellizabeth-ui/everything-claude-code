# Impeccable Critique

Invoke the `critique` mode of the `frontend-design-direction` skill. Give an
honest adversarial review of the current UI's design quality — not just a
checklist pass, but a judgment of whether the design is actually good.

## What Gets Critiqued

- Does the first screen immediately communicate what the product does and for whom?
- Is the visual direction (tone, palette, typography) appropriate to the domain and audience?
- Does anything read as generic, templated, or AI-generated by default?
- Are there missed opportunities to make the design more distinctive or purposeful?
- Does the motion, color, or layout work against the user's workflow rather than supporting it?
- Is the memorable detail — the one idea that makes this feel intentional — present? If not, what should it be?

## Usage

```
/impeccable-critique
/impeccable-critique <component, page, or design decision>
```

## Output Format

```
## Critique: <component or page name>

**What works:** <1-2 sentences on genuine strengths>

**What is weak or generic:**
- <specific issue with a reason>
- ...

**What is wrong:**
- <specific failure with a fix>
- ...

**Recommendation:** <one paragraph on the single most important improvement>
```

Be direct. A good critique names specifics, not generalities. "The color palette
feels bland" is not a critique. "The primary action button uses the same blue as
the background tint, making it invisible at a glance" is.
